Light Painting Stories, 2023

Here are a bunch of really unique light-painted photographs that Joannie and I created in 2023. That year, nearly all of our amazing projects involved a vehicle of some sort. A few of those vehicles are Corvettes. However, there are several other cool cars, some amazing trucks (one of which won the MSRA 2023 Custom Car of the Year!), and even some aircraft mixed in there too! Each light painting project begins with a mini-story followed by the final one-of-a-kind light-painted artwork. Then, the button under each image is linked to “The Rest of the Story” from our sister website’s blog at Michael Anderson Photography. To discover more about any particular image, just click on that button below the photograph, and you'll be taken there! Many of those stories include lots of other photographs, and many also include the setup shot taken prior to the light painting getting underway. There’s also a link to the “Build Video” for each project. Those videos, found on our YouTube channel, feature the images that Michael used to create the final artwork. Then you’ll see the light painting come to life, one layer at a time, in post-production. These projects are all in chronological order. Thank you for taking the time to check them out!

Light Painting a 2017 Corvette Grand Sport in Osage Beach, MissouriHow Brian and Barb's 2017 Corvette Grand Sport light painting project came to be is a great story all by itself! Brian found us when doing a search on the internet for “Vintage Gas Stations”!He wanted to use our cool light painting of Gregg’s Vintage Gas Station in Carver, Minnesota, as the backdrop for his 1927 Spirit T Roadster's display at World of Wheels in Kansas City. The rest is history. The same evening that we created this beautiful light painting of their 2017 Corvette Grand Sport in front of their beautiful home, we light-painted that car, too! It's up next!

Light Painting a 2017 Corvette Grand Sport - The Rest of the Story

Light Painting a 1927 Spirit T RoadsterAs we just mentioned above, we light-painted Brian and Barb’s second car, this stunning 1927 Spirit T Roadster, right after light-painting their 2017 Corvette Grand Sport.As you can see by the sky, rain was on the way, and it was forecast to stick around for the next couple of days. So, without moving the camera, we took the Corvette away and replaced it with this amazing car that Brian built.Doing that would speed up the light painting process, because we would not have to light paint the house and landscaping all over again, just the Roadster, and the areas right around the car. It worked out perfectly, and we finished up shortly before the rain set in!

Light Painting a 1927 Spirit T Roadster - The Full Story

Light Painting the Engine Bay of a 1927 Spirit T RoadsterThe rain did indeed arrive, and it stayed around for a couple of days. I went to bed, as I'd been up nearly 20 hours, driving from Minnesota to Missouri, and then doing two light paintings - all in the same day! Brian had tucked the Roadster into its trailer to keep it nice and dry. Then, while I was sleeping, he moved it to his storage building. In the morning, I asked him where the Roadster was. I wanted to light paint that engine bay!So, we created these two incredible light paintings, right inside the trailer! It was tight quarters, but we got it done!
Light Painting a Harley Davidson Screaming Eagle 103Brian and Barb both drive Harley-Davidson motorcycles. And, while I couldn't talk them into having a portrait created with their bikes, Brian did tell me that I should light paint the engine on his Harley.This is a Screaming Eagle 103 motor, and I must say that it has more than a little bit of chrome!I didn't write a story about this one, as it was created in their garage on a rainy day, because "It was there"!
Light Painting a 2023 Torch Red C8 Corvette Stingray The other reason I went to Missouri was to attend the Lake of the Ozarks Corvette Show. Brian set the whole thing up as a thank you for helping him to create the backdrop for him to use for the Roadster at car shows.While there, Lee and Heidi hired me to create a light painting of their brand-new 2023 Torch Red C8 Corvette Stingray. They were leaving the next day to go back home and wanted to know if I could create it that night, right after the show. Since I was still manning my booth at the Corvette show, I asked them to scout out a location for their project. They discovered the beautiful Seven Springs Winery in nearby Lynn Creek, Missouri. The rest is history.

The Full Story Behind the Light Painting of a 2023 Torch Red C8 Corvette Stingray

Light Painting a 2013 60th Anniversary Corvette Grand Sport 3LT with 3 Jet Aircraft!The Lake of the Ozarks Corvette show was fantastic! The "Best of Show" winner was this stunning 2013 60th Anniversary Corvette Grand Sport 3LT! I light-painted it for owner's, Bobby and Billie, on my way back home to Minnesota. This unique light painting project was created at AVMATS Jet Support at the Spirit of St Louis Airport in Chesterfield, Missouri, where Bobby works.We created five unique light paintings that night. The interior, the amazing engine compartment, both the front and rear end views, and the whole Corvette with 3 corporate jets in the scene! A full night's work, and a very rewarding one at that!

Light Painting a Rare 1969 Dodge DaytonaJust one week later, we had a nice dry evening to light paint this rare car, a mint condition 1969 Dodge Daytona.The owners of the car tell me that it is believed that this is the only one in existence that is a "one-family" owned 1969 Dodge Daytona. Light Painting Wedding Photography at the Chart HouseIn late October of 2023, we had the honor of photographing Jess and Jess' beautiful (and very cold) fall wedding at the Chart House in Lakeville, Minnesota.All told that day, Joannie and I created seven light paintings. Six of those were small still-life images like the two you see below. The final, and by far the most challenging, was the one with Jess and Jess outside by the pond, on the bridge.Once we decided to proceed (because of the freezing temperatures), I selected the pretty pond area. Then we placed our newlyweds on the wooden bridge. Once we had their portraits done, which only took a few minutes, they returned to their wedding dance to warm up and have fun with their guests. Then, Joannie and I got to work! All told, we took 187 images during the photography part. I ended up using 48 of them to create their final artwork. Boy, it sure is pretty!Once we finished the photography, I sent Joannie inside to thaw out. (Did I mention that it was cold?) Then, I carefully gathered all of the goodies we used to create the image. All four strobes and our Canon R5 camera were turned off outside so that they didn’t short out once they got back into the warm air indoors. Then, I left everything in my camera bags for a couple of days to let them get thoroughly acclimated to normal temperatures.It was all in a day's work for a couple of professional wedding photographers.
