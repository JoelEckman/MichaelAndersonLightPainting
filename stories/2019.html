Light Painting Stories, 2019

Here are a couple of dozen unique light-painted photographs that we created in 2019. Each one has a mini-story followed by the final artwork. Then, the button under the image is linked to “The Rest of the Story", which is found on our sister website at Michael Anderson Photography. To discover more about any particular image, just click on that button below the photograph, and you'll be taken there! Many of those stories include other photographs, including the setup shot taken prior to the light painting getting started. There’s also a link to the “Build Video” for each project. Those videos, found on our YouTube channel, feature the images that Michael used to create the final artwork. Then you’ll see the light painting come to life, one layer at a time, in post-production. Once again, these light-painted photographs were all created in 2019 and are in chronological order. Enjoy!

Drag the line across any photo with a setup shot to see the scene before it was light painted.

Light-Painted Wedding Photograph at the White Bear Yacht Club

Yes, another wedding light painting! At this point, we'd only been light painting for a little over two years. Many of our unique light paintings were created at weddings, well, because we were photographing them nearly every weekend! At the time, I had also started to display our light-painted photographs at car shows to get more vehicles to light paint. Car shows were starting to generate some good leads, too. But what we did have going for us was weddings, and lots of them! So we kept practicing and refining our craft every chance we could. What makes this particular wedding light painting even more unique is that it was created in broad daylight! James and Erin's wedding day was complete by 3:00 pm. Yep, their pretty outdoor ceremony on White Bear Lake, and their wonderful luncheon afterward, all finished up almost six hours before the sunset! Well, we had a streak going! We'd created a light painting at every wedding for nearly two years, including weddings with blizzards and sub-zero weather! We weren't going to not light paint at a wedding if I could help it! It's a good thing that we own a lot of powerful strobes! They were just the ticket to getting it to look dark, even though it wasn't.
