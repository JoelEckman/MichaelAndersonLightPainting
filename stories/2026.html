Light Painting Stories, 2026

These are the mini-stories about the cool light painting photography projects that Joannie and I have created thus far in 2026. Summer's not even over, and we’ve already created a bunch of amazing projects! So far, we’ve light-painted in Georgia (5 projects), Florida, Missouri, Arkansas (2 projects), Oregon (3 projects), Wisconsin (4 projects), and back home in Minnesota (3 projects). Each of these wonderful light paintings is listed in chronological order. To learn more about a particular project, just click on the button below each one, and you'll be taken to our sister website’s blog at Michael Anderson Photography for "The Rest of the Story”. This page will be a work in progress. We’ll keep adding projects to it as we complete them throughout the year. Each project will also be added to the appropriate gallery on our Home Page. Please check back often to see what we’ve been up to! Once again in 2026, we hit the road. In late January, we headed to Florida to exhibit our Light Painting Photography at the amazing Cars on 5th Concourse in Naples, Florida. This was our second year at the show, and we loved it! We also had a light painting project lined up in Georgia while on our way to Florida. This project was of two WWII vehicles, a Piper L-4 N33579 and a Willys “MB” Jeep! It was a fun project that turned out very cool! The morning after that one, we continued on to Florida. After a lot of fun at Disney World and then some relaxation with friends in Fort Myers, Joannie and I headed to Naples for the Cars on 5th weekend. At the Friday evening Jetport Reception, we booked our very first light painting of a Porsche! This was not just any Porsche, either; this rare 1958 Porsche 356A 1600 Speedster is a real stunner! We would light paint it a week after the show. On our way back home to Minnesota, we would light paint four more amazing projects in Georgia. The first one was an AirCam experimental aircraft, and then, on the same night, we light-painted its cool Twin Rotax 912S engines. The following day, Joannie and I headed to the Army Aviation Heritage Foundation & Flying Museum. While there, we would light paint both their amazing AH-1F “Cobra” N826HF helicopter and their UH-1 “Huey” helicopter at their hangar in Hampton, GA. Then, in late April, we headed to Missouri for the wonderful “Corvettes in Paradise” show put on by the Lake of the Ozarks Corvette Club. While there, we light-painted a beautiful 2014 Stingray Convertible. We also booked two other projects in Arkansas. We would be headed there right after the show. In Arkansas, on the first night, we light-painted an amazing 1967 C2 Corvette Resto-Mod Coupe. Its “Hot” Razzleberry paint job is simply amazing! The very next evening, we were near Austin, Arkansas. There, we light-painted a pair of red, white, and blue Corvettes in one very patriotic image! In that unique light painting, we had a 2019 Admiral Blue Corvette Grand Sport as well as a 1996 Admiral Blue Corvette Grand Sport. Shortly after getting back home, I flew out to Eugene, Oregon, and the Soaring By The Sea Foundation. This project came up very suddenly, and so Joannie wasn’t able to clear her schedule to go along. So my trusty assistant that night was Coy Pfaff, the foundation's Executive Director! Coy and I light-painted their amazing 1943 P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk, along with a WWII Willys Jeep. This aircraft and Jeep are so cool that he and I light-painted them two different ways! The next morning, I had about 3 hours before my flight back home. So, just for the fun of it, I light-painted another of their amazing aircraft, a 1952 AD-4N Skyraider that was undergoing routine maintenance. Because it was daylight, and inside the hangar was very bright, I light-painted it using just a flash. It turned out surprisingly well, considering how bright it was in there! Back home in Minnesota, Joannie and I had an epic project lined up - The Batmobile! That night, we light-painted the Batmobile from three different angles, using 3 cameras. I honestly cannot decide which version is my favorite! In early July, we headed to Oshkosh, Wisconsin, but not for AirVeture, that wasn't until later in the month. Nope, we were there for a special project that literally evolved right up until we did the photography! Originally, we were there to light paint a Ford Bronco Heritage Edition and a Ferrari Roma Spider. The theme for this project was going to be "Ford vs. Ferrari". This unique combination of automobiles was the brainchild of Jennifer Jensen. I'd met Jennifer, the President of NextJen Studios, at Oshkosh back in 2024. Her company produces the TV show "The Aviators" and also "Auto Exotica" magazine. She loved what we do, and we talked about doing a light painting that she could feature in Auto Exotica. Well, this was the light painting! What we didn't find out, until about an hour before we set up for the shoot, was that there might be an aircraft available to include in the light painting. With AirVenture just a little more than a week away, a P-51D Mustang was already in town for the show. And, it was being stored in the hangar that we were using for the Ford vs Ferrari project. After a couple of phone calls, Jennifer got permission to have that cool aircraft, "Happy Jack's Go Buggy" in the light painting with the Bronco and the Ferrari! So cool! We created multiple versions of the cars with the P-51. And a couple of versions of the Mustang all by itself. Each one of them turned out so amazing! Please check out the cars in our "Cars, Trucks, and Motorcycles" gallery. "Happy Jack's Go Buggy" is featured in our "Light-Painted Aircraft and More" gallery. We departed Oshkosh and headed north to Superior, Wisconsin, for two more amazing light painting projects. The first, a cool 1941 Chevy AK pickup truck, was created at the remains of the Twohy Mercantile Building. That building was destroyed by a fire a few years ago, but its brick shell is still (mostly) intact. It made for a wonderful backdrop for the Chevy! The following night, we headed about an hour or so away to Delta Diner in Mason, Wisconsin. It's there that we light-painted a stunning purple and white 1957 Pontiac Chieftain! That image will be done very soon and added to this website. About one week later, we headed right back to Oshkosh for EAA Airventure 2026! Our Light Painting Photography booth is located in Hangar C, Row F, Booth 3064. We light-painted an amazing aircraft there during the Wednesday night airshow. That image, and its story, will be added here once it's completed. We also booked new projects that we will be getting scheduled soon. So far, we’re only into August! We have several projects lined up this month, with others in the pipeline for September. In the meantime, thank you for taking the time out of your day to check out our light painting passion. We’ll be back with more cool light painting projects soon! - M&J So, without further ado, here we go!

Light Painting AH-1F “Cobra” N826HFThe night after light-painting the AirCam, Joannie and I headed to Hampton, Georgia, where we had an amazing project lined up to light-paint a pair of helicopters! This one, of AH-1F “Cobra” N826HF, and a UH-1H "Huey" N426HF. Both helicopter light painting projects were created in the hangar at the Army Aviation Heritage Foundation & Flying Museum in Hampton, Georgia. However, after assessing the size of these helicopters (they are really big), we chose to light-paint each one separately. By doing so, it makes each one the star of the show and, of course, much larger in the imagery. The history of each of these helicopters is amazing! The Cobra was shot down twice in Vietnam (the pilot died in the first one). Please click on the button below to read more about Cobra N826HF light painting and its storied history, too.As a side note, you can take a ride on both of these helicopters! The Army Aviation Heritage Foundation takes both of them to airshows throughout the country where they're both on display, and you can take a ride on them, too!

Light Painting AH-1F “Cobra” N826HF

Light Painting UH-1H "Huey" N426HFWhile the Cobra is a large helicopter, the Huey is even bigger! It has a total length of approximately 44 feet from rotor to tail! It’s about 14 feet tall, with a 48-foot main rotor diameter, and it weighs in at about 9,500 pounds! Yep, it's a big one!Like the Cobra, this Huey is also a Vietnam-era helicopter. While it didn't actually serve in combat, it did its service behind the scenes in several parts of the world.And, as mentioned above, you can ride this wonderful aircraft at airshows too!

Light Painting a 1943 P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk with a WWII Willys JeepJust a short time after arriving back home from our light painting trip to Missouri and Arkansas, I received a text from our friend Ray Fowler. Ray was heading to Eugene, Oregon, and wondered if we could meet him there that week to light paint a WWII Warbird that he had been piloting for a documentary. Joannie was unavailable on such short notice, but I managed to make it work on my schedule! And, boy, am I glad I could go! The location for this amazing project was at Soaring By The Sea Foundation, right there at Mahlon Sweet Field in Eugene. The subject: a P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk! This cool Warbird has a very storied history.Since Joannie wasn't there, I ended up recruiting Coy Pfaff, the foundation's Executive Director, as my trusty assistant. Coy came through with flying colors (pun intended)! Coy and I light-painted these two WWII vehicles right out in front of the foundation's hangar. While we were at it, we also created two unique versions, one up high (this one) and another, very low to the ground. A scissors lift allowed us to get the camera and tripod up high for this one.

Light Painting a 1943 P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk with a WWII Willys Jeep

Light Painting a Restored 1943 P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk with a WWII Willys Jeep - Take 2This second version of the fully-restored Kittyhawk, created right after the first, was taken with a second camera from a different, and much lower, angle.After being recovered from a remote airfield in Tadji, New Guinea, in 1973, the P-40 was brought back to flying condition in the 1990's, by Pioneer Aero Restorations in Auckland, New Zealand.In 2024, Soaring by the Sea Foundation acquired the Kittyhawk and imported it to the United States. The restored P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk was then painted in original 75 Squadron RAAF camouflage. This remarkable Warbird has six 50-caliber M2 Browning machine guns (one of them is on the ground next to ammo boxes and the Jeep). Those six Browning machine guns are fully functional and can fire up to 3000 rounds in under a minute!

Light Painting a Restored 1943 P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk with a WWII Willys Jeep

Light Painting a 1952 AD-4N SkyraiderThe morning after light painting both versions of the P-40 N-1 Kittyhawk, I had a few hours of free time before catching my flight back home. So, why not have a little creative fun with another of the cool aircraft in the hangar at Soaring By The Sea Foundation?Their amazing 1952 AD-4N Skyraider was undergoing routine maintenance in the hangar. Much of the engine compartment was open, which exposed a lot of cool details. All of that detail would make for an amazing light painting! I had time on my hands, so why not give it a try? I asked, and received permission to have a go at it.Because it was daytime, the hangar interior was very bright. So the only option was to light-paint it using a strobe light. This light painting technique creates a slightly different look than long exposure light painting, but it captures details very nicely!  This "spur-of-the-moment" project turned out pretty cool, especially considering how bright it was inside the hangar that morning!The large American Flag was indeed displayed on the wall of the hangar. In post-production, I took artistic license and enlarged it to fill the entire background.We introduced the final artwork on July 4th, 2026, on America's 250th Birthday!
