How Is It Done?

A typical light painting project involves the use of specially modified LED lights. In many cases, some of the exposures are also created using our high-powered strobe lights. This page will give you a good idea of what will go into creating your light-painted artwork. Here, you’ll find several completed light paintings, as well as their "Build Videos". Michael creates one of these videos for most projects and places it on their YouTube channel. These short videos start out with the images that were actually used to create each final one-of-a-kind light painting. Then, you’ll see the final image “come to life”, one small piece at a time! Most of the time, Michael takes between 150 and 250 images (sometimes many more) to be sure everything is covered with light from multiple angles. A typical light painting project takes 3 to 4 hours to photograph. And the average post-production time is 12 to 16 hours using Photoshop and several other programs to complete the final artwork.

Michael met Dave at Motorhead Madness in Duluth, Minnesota, in the winter of 2024. His 1934 Ford 3-Window Coupe was selected as the "Best of Show", and for good reason! Nicknamed "POISIN", this automobile is absolutely stunning! You could eat off the underside of this car - it's that clean!

The last few years at Motorhead Madness, Michael has given a light painting gift certificate to the "Best of Show" winner. Dave decided to "Go big, or go home", with an 8-vehicle light painting that featured their beautiful home as the backdrop.

Dave has names for each of his vehicles, and those names are on the license plates of each one!

The 3 Motorcycles are: "KEEPIN", a 1995 Custom Harley-Davidson Road King, "FLOWIN", a Custom 2005 Speedway Chopper, and "FLAMIN", a 2022 CVO Harley-Davidson.

The other 5 Vehicles are: "POISIN", a ’34 Ford 3-Window Coupe, "TOOLIN", a Marina Blue 1967 Chevelle, "GLOWIN", a 2023 Z3 Corvette Stingray Coupe 3LT, "KICKIN", a 2024 Chevy Blazer AWD, and "HAULIN", a 2023 Chevy Tahoe 4WD RST.

Michael took more than 550 images that night, and nearly 200 of them were used to create Dave's truly one-of-a-kind artwork! There are nearly 40 hours of post-production in this one as well.
